# Years 7–10 — English topics

Source of truth: Australian Curriculum v9.0, English F–10 (strands: Language LA,
Literature LE, Literacy LY). Every content description for Years 7–10 is covered
below and mirrored in `apps/mobile/src/data/year{7,8,9,10}/english.ts`.

Per AC v9.0, Years 7–8 each carry **23** content descriptions (Language 9,
Literature 5, Literacy 9); Year 9 carries **23** (Language 9, Literature 6,
Literacy 8) and Year 10 carries **25** (Language 9, Literature 8, Literacy 8).
The strand totals below follow the real curriculum, not the earlier-year split.

Coverage notes: Years 9–10 relocate "word knowledge" — Year 9's
`AC9E9LY08` covers spelling **for effect**, and Year 10's `AC9E10LA08` covers
technical/academic vocabulary in the Language strand — so the Literacy strand
shifts to 8 CDs per year while Literature grows.

## Year 7 — English topics

### Y7-ENG-LA01 · Language variation — dialect, register & jargon
- CDs: `AC9E7LA01`
- Learn: language varies by speaker and situation — Australian slang, courtroom
  English, surfer jargon and dialects are all valid varieties; register is
  formality for the situation, dialect is tied to origin/community.
- Illustrations: a formality thermometer sliding from mates-talk to courtroom
  English.
- Assignment: 6 questions (nominal) / 4 (compact) — identify varieties, shift register.

### Y7-ENG-LA02 · Persuasive & evaluative language
- CDs: `AC9E7LA02`
- Learn: evaluative words ("outstanding", "disastrous") nudge judgement without
  seeming to argue; reading critically means asking whether a word states a fact
  or pushes an opinion.
- Illustrations: a balance scale weighing a neutral word against a loaded,
  evaluative word.
- Assignment: 6/4 — spot evaluative words, identify persuasive intent.

### Y7-ENG-LA03 · Text structures across genres
- CDs: `AC9E7LA03`
- Learn: sophisticated texts blend structures — a feature article mixes narrative
  and fact, a blog mixes opinion and instructions; recognising hybrid structures
  helps predict and judge purpose.
- Illustrations: a flowchart of a hybrid feature-article structure.
- Assignment: 6/4 — identify hybrid structures, match stages to purpose.

### Y7-ENG-LA04 · Cohesion — paragraphing & cohesive devices
- CDs: `AC9E7LA04`
- Learn: a paragraph opens with a topic sentence and every other sentence
  supports it; cohesive devices (however, therefore, as a result) act as
  signposts linking ideas.
- Illustrations: a paragraph flowchart with topic-sentence hub and supporting
  sentence spokes.
- Assignment: 6/4 — find topic sentences, insert cohesive devices.

### Y7-ENG-LA05 · Clause combining — compound & complex sentences
- CDs: `AC9E7LA05`
- Learn: compound sentences join two independent ideas; complex sentences join a
  main idea to a dependent clause (because, although, when); the choice signals
  the relationship between ideas.
- Illustrations: two sentence-building blocks — equal halves joined vs a leaning
  dependent clause.
- Assignment: 6/4 — classify and build compound/complex sentences.

### Y7-ENG-LA06 · Modality — showing certainty & obligation
- CDs: `AC9E7LA06`
- Learn: modal verbs and adverbs dial certainty up or down (will/might/must,
  certainly/possibly); writers choose modality to stay honest about how sure they
  are.
- Illustrations: a certainty scale from impossible to certain marked with modal
  words.
- Assignment: 6/4 — rank certainty, choose modals for accuracy.

### Y7-ENG-LA07 · Multimodal & visual design features
- CDs: `AC9E7LA07`
- Learn: layout, font size, colour and image placement shape meaning; multimodal
  texts combine words, images and sound so each mode adds something the others
  cannot.
- Illustrations: a webpage layout with headline, image and body text labelled by
  design choice.
- Assignment: 6/4 — explain the effect of layout and design choices.

### Y7-ENG-LA08 · Figurative language — extended metaphor & symbolism
- CDs: `AC9E7LA08`
- Learn: an extended metaphor develops one comparison across a whole text; a
  symbol is an object standing for a bigger idea; allusion references another
  well-known text.
- Illustrations: a journey-as-life extended metaphor traced across a winding road.
- Assignment: 6/4 — track extended metaphors, decode symbols and allusions.

### Y7-ENG-LA09 · Punctuation for clarity & effect — semicolon, colon & dash
- CDs: `AC9E7LA09`
- Learn: semicolons join closely related independent sentences, colons introduce
  lists or explanations, dashes create dramatic pauses; each mark makes a craft
  choice about rhythm and emphasis.
- Illustrations: three punctuation marks shown with their distinct jobs.
- Assignment: 6/4 — insert and justify advanced punctuation.

### Y7-ENG-LE01 · Responding to literature across contexts
- CDs: `AC9E7LE01`
- Learn: stories carry the fingerprints of when and where they were made; reading
  literature from diverse historical and cultural contexts — including First
  Nations Australian authors — builds empathy.
- Illustrations: a world map with book icons marking diverse story origins.
- Assignment: 6/4 — connect context to character choices and events.

### Y7-ENG-LE02 · Comparing texts across forms
- CDs: `AC9E7LE02`
- Learn: the same theme appears differently in a poem, short story and film; each
  form shapes meaning through its own tools (compressed images vs actors'
  expressions and music).
- Illustrations: a bar-compare of how much space three forms give to a shared
  theme.
- Assignment: 6/4 — compare how forms convey a shared theme.

### Y7-ENG-LE03 · An author's craft — structure & language choices
- CDs: `AC9E7LE03`
- Learn: authors make deliberate structural and language choices — starting
  mid-action, withholding a name, short tense sentences; every choice is a clue
  to meaning.
- Illustrations: a flowchart showing how a craft choice leads to a reader effect.
- Assignment: 6/4 — explain the effect of specific craft choices.

### Y7-ENG-LE04 · Poetic techniques — form & sound
- CDs: `AC9E7LE04`
- Learn: a poem's form (free verse, sonnet, ballad) shapes meaning; sound devices
  (assonance, consonance, rhythm) work alongside imagery to make poetry felt.
- Illustrations: a ladder of poetic forms from free verse to strict rhyme scheme.
- Assignment: 6/4 — identify forms and sound devices, explain their effect.

### Y7-ENG-LE05 · Creating literary texts — experimenting with viewpoint
- CDs: `AC9E7LE05`
- Learn: point of view is a lens (first/second/third person); rewriting a
  familiar scene from a new viewpoint reveals what that lens can do.
- Illustrations: a camera-lens metaphor for first, second and third person
  viewpoints.
- Assignment: 6/4 — rewrite scenes in different viewpoints, explain effects.

### Y7-ENG-LY01 · Analysing representation in texts
- CDs: `AC9E7LY01`
- Learn: every text represents its subject through choices — who is shown, who is
  left out; representation is never neutral.
- Illustrations: a camera frame showing what is included vs cropped out.
- Assignment: 6/4 — analyse what is shown and omitted in a representation.

### Y7-ENG-LY02 · Listening & interacting in discussion
- CDs: `AC9E7LY02`
- Learn: good discussion means genuinely listening, then extending or
  respectfully challenging ideas; the best contributions connect to what came
  before.
- Illustrations: a discussion chain where each speaker links back to the last.
- Assignment: 6/4 — identify building-on and respectful-challenge moves.

### Y7-ENG-LY03 · Structure & language features working together
- CDs: `AC9E7LY03`
- Learn: structure and language features are a team — a persuasive speech builds
  reasons and uses rhetorical questions to match; analyse purpose by checking
  they pull the same way.
- Illustrations: a flowchart linking structure choice, language choice and
  audience effect.
- Assignment: 6/4 — analyse alignment of structure, language and purpose.

### Y7-ENG-LY04 · Reading strategy — navigating a range of texts
- CDs: `AC9E7LY04`
- Learn: match reading strategy to purpose — skim for overview, scan for one
  fact, read closely for detail; efficient readers vary their speed.
- Illustrations: a flowchart choosing a reading strategy based on purpose.
- Assignment: 6/4 — choose the right strategy for a given purpose.

### Y7-ENG-LY05 · Comprehension strategies — monitoring & inferring
- CDs: `AC9E7LY05`
- Learn: monitor meaning and pause to re-read, look up words or question;
  inferring combines text clues with prior knowledge.
- Illustrations: a reader pausing mid-page with a question mark, then re-reading.
- Assignment: 6/4 — infer meaning, choose the best next step when meaning breaks.

### Y7-ENG-LY06 · Planning, drafting & publishing persuasive texts
- CDs: `AC9E7LY06`
- Learn: a persuasive text is built — plan reasons, draft the argument, revise
  for evaluative language and cohesion, then polish for the audience.
- Illustrations: a persuasive-writing process wheel: plan → draft → revise →
  publish.
- Assignment: 6/4 — sequence the process, revise language and evidence.

### Y7-ENG-LY07 · Planning & delivering spoken presentations
- CDs: `AC9E7LY07`
- Learn: a spoken presentation needs planning plus performance choices — pitch,
  tone, pace, volume and eye contact; rehearse aloud to catch awkward phrasing.
- Illustrations: a rehearsal cycle: plan, practise aloud, get feedback, refine.
- Assignment: 6/4 — plan delivery, choose performance choices for effect.

### Y7-ENG-LY08 · Word knowledge — morphemes & etymology
- CDs: `AC9E7LY08`
- Learn: morphemes are the smallest units of meaning (un- + break + -able);
  etymology traces a word's history from Latin, Greek, French and other languages.
- Illustrations: a word broken into morpheme building blocks.
- Assignment: 6/4 — split words into morphemes, use origins to decode meaning.

### Y7-ENG-LY09 · Building technical & subject-specific vocabulary
- CDs: `AC9E7LY09`
- Learn: every subject has precise vocabulary — photosynthesis, variable,
  colonisation; using the exact term shows real understanding.
- Illustrations: a vocabulary notebook collecting subject-specific terms.
- Assignment: 6/4 — match technical terms to subjects, use them precisely.

## Year 8 — English topics

### Y8-ENG-LA01 · Language, identity & power
- CDs: `AC9E8LA01`
- Learn: the way someone speaks signals community, generation and background; a
  "standard" form is one socially favoured variety among many, and language
  carries power over whose voice is taken seriously.
- Illustrations: a balance scale weighing "standard" English against a community
  dialect.
- Assignment: 6/4 — analyse identity signals and the social power of language.

### Y8-ENG-LA02 · Rhetorical devices
- CDs: `AC9E8LA02`
- Learn: rhetorical devices persuade — repetition drums ideas in, the rule of
  three makes lists feel complete, rhetorical questions prompt silent agreement.
- Illustrations: three rhetorical devices shown with a famous-style example each.
- Assignment: 6/4 — identify and explain the effect of each device.

### Y8-ENG-LA03 · Text structures adapted for purpose
- CDs: `AC9E8LA03`
- Learn: writers bend familiar structures — a listicle borrows a report's order
  and adds humour; a podcast script is built to be heard; each adaptation is a
  trade-off.
- Illustrations: a flowchart showing a report structure adapted into a listicle.
- Assignment: 6/4 — explain how and why structures are adapted.

### Y8-ENG-LA04 · Cohesion — linking paragraphs & nominalisation
- CDs: `AC9E8LA04`
- Learn: whole paragraphs need linking; nominalisation turns a verb into a noun
  ("expanded" → "expansion") to refer back to an idea concisely.
- Illustrations: a chain linking paragraph endings to the next paragraph's
  opening idea.
- Assignment: 6/4 — link paragraphs, nominalise verbs.

### Y8-ENG-LA05 · Complex sentences — embedding for elaboration
- CDs: `AC9E8LA05`
- Learn: embedding packs extra information into a sentence without a new
  sentence; stacking clauses builds density but balance is key for clarity.
- Illustrations: a sentence with a nested embedded clause shown as a box within
  a box.
- Assignment: 6/4 — identify and build embedded-clause sentences.

### Y8-ENG-LA06 · Nominalisation & abstract nouns for tone
- CDs: `AC9E8LA06`
- Learn: abstract nouns name ideas (justice, freedom, growth); leaning on them
  and nominalisation gives writing a formal, objective, official tone.
- Illustrations: a concrete verb sentence rewritten as an abstract nominalised
  sentence.
- Assignment: 6/4 — rewrite concretes as abstracts, analyse tone.

### Y8-ENG-LA07 · Multimodal texts — visual & audio interplay
- CDs: `AC9E8LA07`
- Learn: music can contradict visuals on purpose (cheerful music over a sad
  scene creates irony); analyse whether modes reinforce or clash.
- Illustrations: a scene where cheerful music plays against a sad image, creating
  irony.
- Assignment: 6/4 — analyse mode interactions and their effects.

### Y8-ENG-LA08 · Figurative language & extended symbolism
- CDs: `AC9E8LA08`
- Learn: a single symbol can shift meaning across a text (a caged bird from
  sadness to hope); tracking figurative evolution shows deeper analysis.
- Illustrations: a symbol's meaning shifting across a story's beginning, middle
  and end.
- Assignment: 6/4 — track symbol development, explain allusions.

### Y8-ENG-LA09 · Punctuation in complex sentences
- CDs: `AC9E8LA09`
- Learn: commas mark embedded clauses, semicolons separate related items, brackets
  add asides; punctuation in a complex sentence is a map for the reader's
  attention.
- Illustrations: a long complex sentence annotated with punctuation roles.
- Assignment: 6/4 — punctuate complex sentences correctly.

### Y8-ENG-LE01 · Literature reflecting diverse identities
- CDs: `AC9E8LE01`
- Learn: literature from diverse identities, times and cultures — including First
  Nations Australian and world authors — expands whose experience is central.
- Illustrations: a bookshelf of diverse authors and perspectives.
- Assignment: 6/4 — recognise and value perspectives beyond your own.

### Y8-ENG-LE02 · Comparing representations of an issue
- CDs: `AC9E8LE02`
- Learn: two texts about the same real issue can represent it differently; ask
  whose feelings and values are centred and whose are in the background.
- Illustrations: two book covers on the same theme compared side by side.
- Assignment: 6/4 — compare representations, explain choices.

### Y8-ENG-LE03 · How authors position readers
- CDs: `AC9E8LE03`
- Learn: authors control what we know and when to shape our judgement — a late
  backstory reveal can shift sympathy; structure and language position us.
- Illustrations: a flowchart showing a reveal shifting reader sympathy.
- Assignment: 6/4 — analyse how positioning techniques steer judgement.

### Y8-ENG-LE04 · Poetic & dramatic techniques for effect
- CDs: `AC9E8LE04`
- Learn: in drama, pauses, spotlights and asides carry meaning; in poetry,
  enjambment creates momentum and mid-line full stops force an abrupt halt.
- Illustrations: a stage with a spotlight and an aside labelled for dramatic
  effect.
- Assignment: 6/4 — explain staging and line-break choices.

### Y8-ENG-LE05 · Creating texts that experiment with structure & voice
- CDs: `AC9E8LE05`
- Learn: structure need not run in order — flashbacks build suspense; voice is a
  narrator's personality on the page.
- Illustrations: a story timeline reordered: ending shown first, then flashback.
- Assignment: 6/4 — experiment with non-chronological structure and voice.

### Y8-ENG-LY01 · Analysing representation for audience
- CDs: `AC9E8LY01`
- Learn: the same event is shaped differently for different audiences — a
  children's news bulletin softens what a tabloid sensationalises.
- Illustrations: the same event reported for two different audiences, compared.
- Assignment: 6/4 — compare how audience shapes representation.

### Y8-ENG-LY02 · Effective discussion, debate & collaboration
- CDs: `AC9E8LY02`
- Learn: a debate has opening, rebuttal and closing roles; collaborative
  discussion shares airtime fairly and builds shared understanding.
- Illustrations: a debate structure: opening, rebuttal, closing.
- Assignment: 6/4 — sequence a debate, identify collaborative moves.

### Y8-ENG-LY03 · Structure & language across modes
- CDs: `AC9E8LY03`
- Learn: the same content is reshaped for print, audio or video; each mode needs
  different structural tools (headings vs verbal signposting vs visual cues).
- Illustrations: the same content adapted across print, audio and video modes.
- Assignment: 6/4 — adapt content across modes, analyse structural tools.

### Y8-ENG-LY04 · Reading strategy for complex texts
- CDs: `AC9E8LY04`
- Learn: dense and archaic texts need slowing down, chunking, context clues and
  knowing when to reread or keep going.
- Illustrations: a decision flowchart for tackling a dense or archaic sentence.
- Assignment: 6/4 — apply strategies to complex texts.

### Y8-ENG-LY05 · Comprehension — synthesising across texts
- CDs: `AC9E8LY05`
- Learn: synthesising combines several sources into one understanding; noticing
  and explaining disagreement between sources builds a critical view.
- Illustrations: multiple source icons combining into a single understanding icon.
- Assignment: 6/4 — synthesise sources, explain explicit vs implicit meaning.

### Y8-ENG-LY06 · Planning, drafting & editing for purpose
- CDs: `AC9E8LY06`
- Learn: editing checks that every paragraph serves the purpose; separate passes
  for structure, then language, then punctuation catch more.
- Illustrations: three separate editing passes: structure, language, then
  punctuation.
- Assignment: 6/4 — sequence editing passes, revise for purpose.

### Y8-ENG-LY07 · Spoken presentations using rhetorical choices
- CDs: `AC9E8LY07`
- Learn: a strong talk deploys rhetorical choices deliberately — a rule-of-three
  list, a pause before a statistic, a striking opener and memorable close.
- Illustrations: a speech structure: striking opener, build the case, memorable
  close.
- Assignment: 6/4 — plan rhetorical choices for a presentation.

### Y8-ENG-LY08 · Word knowledge — morphology & etymology for spelling
- CDs: `AC9E8LY08`
- Learn: suffixes like "-tion" build word families (act → action); etymology
  explains "odd" spellings like psychology's silent "p" from Greek.
- Illustrations: a word family tree built from one root with different suffixes.
- Assignment: 6/4 — build word families, explain spellings via origin.

### Y8-ENG-LY09 · Building subject-specific vocabulary
- CDs: `AC9E8LY09`
- Learn: advanced subjects need precise vocabulary — hypothesis, coefficient,
  perspective; strong technical vocabulary lets complex ideas be expressed
  concisely.
- Illustrations: a vocabulary bridge connecting a vague phrase to its precise
  technical term.
- Assignment: 6/4 — match technical terms, explain their precision.

## Year 9 — English topics

### Y9-ENG-LA01 · Language, power, relationships & roles
- CDs: `AC9E9LA01`
- Learn: language can lift someone up or shut them down — jargon, titles, pronouns
  and who gets to interrupt build the unspoken power structure of a conversation.
- Illustrations: a power ladder showing how language can elevate or diminish a
  speaker's role.
- Assignment: 6/4 — recognise how language empowers or diminishes roles.

### Y9-ENG-LA02 · Evaluating — directly & indirectly
- CDs: `AC9E9LA02`
- Learn: evaluation can be direct ("brilliant") or indirect via allusion,
  evocative vocabulary and metaphor; indirect evaluation often persuades
  hardest.
- Illustrations: a balance scale showing direct evaluation vs indirect devices
  (allusion, metaphor).
- Assignment: 6/4 — identify direct vs indirect evaluation devices.

### Y9-ENG-LA03 · Adapting & subverting text structures
- CDs: `AC9E9LA03`
- Learn: authors bend structures — a satirical site borrows a serious news format
  to mock it; subversion flips audience expectations across spoken, written,
  visual and multimodal elements.
- Illustrations: a flowchart: expected structure, then the author's subverted
  twist.
- Assignment: 6/4 — analyse how and why structures are adapted or subverted.

### Y9-ENG-LA04 · Cohesion — condensing & linking ideas
- CDs: `AC9E9LA04`
- Learn: nominalisation condenses an action into a noun; text connectives
  (furthermore, by contrast, as a result) stitch the condensed ideas together
  into dense, fluent writing.
- Illustrations: packing a full idea into one noun, then linking it with a
  connective.
- Assignment: 6/4 — nominalise ideas and link them with connectives.

### Y9-ENG-LA05 · Sentence variation for effect
- CDs: `AC9E9LA05`
- Learn: writers vary sentence structures deliberately — a single short sentence
  or a fragment like "No. Never." breaks the pattern for emphasis, pace or voice.
- Illustrations: a rhythm graph showing long sentences suddenly broken by a short
  fragment.
- Assignment: 6/4 — explain the effect of sentence and fragment variation.

### Y9-ENG-LA06 · Abstract nouns & nominalisation to summarise
- CDs: `AC9E9LA06`
- Learn: abstract nouns name ideas (progress, conflict, innovation);
  nominalisation turns an action into one of these nouns to summarise a whole
  idea and build on it.
- Illustrations: a long sentence folded into one abstract noun, then expanded
  again.
- Assignment: 6/4 — nominalise and summarise ideas precisely.

### Y9-ENG-LA07 · Symbols in still & moving images
- CDs: `AC9E9LA07`
- Learn: an image symbol carries a meaning beyond itself; in moving images
  symbols develop as a colour or setting recurs, augmenting meaning across the
  film.
- Illustrations: a film-strip of one symbol (a door) meaning different things
  across scenes.
- Assignment: 6/4 — decode and track visual symbols.

### Y9-ENG-LA08 · Vocabulary, style, mood & tone
- CDs: `AC9E9LA08`
- Learn: mood is the feeling a text creates, tone is the writer's attitude; both
  are built word by word through vocabulary choices and their shades of meaning.
- Illustrations: a thermometer of mood from cold neutral words to warm evocative
  ones.
- Assignment: 6/4 — analyse how vocabulary creates style, mood and tone.

### Y9-ENG-LA09 · Punctuation for referencing & citing
- CDs: `AC9E9LA09`
- Learn: punctuation is the grammar of giving credit — quotation marks mark exact
  words, brackets frame citations; knowing when to quote vs report keeps writing
  honest.
- Illustrations: an annotated citation showing quotation marks, brackets and
  reference punctuation.
- Assignment: 6/4 — punctuate citations, quote vs report sources.

### Y9-ENG-LE01 · Representations of people & places
- CDs: `AC9E9LE01`
- Learn: literature by First Nations Australian and world authors represents
  people and places through cultural lenses; analyse how an author's culture and
  era shaped what was shown.
- Illustrations: a world map with book icons marking representations from
  different contexts.
- Assignment: 6/4 — analyse and compare cultural representations.

### Y9-ENG-LE02 · First impressions vs whole-text analysis
- CDs: `AC9E9LE02`
- Learn: a personal response compares your initial impression with what close
  reading of the whole text reveals — endings re-read beginnings.
- Illustrations: a flowchart: initial impression, then whole-text re-reading
  changes it.
- Assignment: 6/4 — trace how a response develops across a whole text.

### Y9-ENG-LE03 · Why readers prefer certain texts
- CDs: `AC9E9LE03`
- Learn: readers prefer texts for features built into them — plot, voice, ideas,
  characters; analysing appeal separates the features from personal taste.
- Illustrations: a bar-compare of text features that drive reader preference.
- Assignment: 6/4 — explain what makes texts appealing to different readers.

### Y9-ENG-LE04 · Aesthetic qualities & author style
- CDs: `AC9E9LE04`
- Learn: aesthetic qualities are a text's artistic properties; an author's style
  is the fingerprint of their aesthetic choices — rhythm, imagery, tone.
- Illustrations: a ladder of aesthetic appreciation: plain, skilful, striking.
- Assignment: 6/4 — evaluate an author's style with cited choices.

### Y9-ENG-LE05 · Extended metaphor, allegory & intertextuality
- CDs: `AC9E9LE05`
- Learn: devices can run through a whole text — extended metaphor, metonymy
  ("the crown"), allegory (a whole story with a hidden meaning) and intertextual

- Illustrations: an allegory shown as two overlapping layers: literal story and

- Assignment: 6/4 — identify and explain layered literary devices.

### Y9-ENG-LE06 · Creating hybrid literary texts
- CDs: `AC9E9LE06`
- Learn: a hybrid text mixes conventions from more than one form (narrative +

- Illustrations: a flowchart: choose forms to blend, then edit the hybrid for

- Assignment: 6/4 — plan, create and edit a hybrid literary text.

### Y9-ENG-LY01 · How representations reflect contexts
- CDs: `AC9E9LY01`

- Assignment: 6/4 — connect representations to their contexts.

### Y9-ENG-LY02 · Listening critically to spoken texts
- CDs: `AC9E9LY02`
- Learn: spoken texts position listeners through tone, repetition and timing;

### Y9-ENG-LY03 · Language features representing a perspective
- CDs: `AC9E9LY03`

### Y9-ENG-LY04 · Organising ideas across paragraphs
- CDs: `AC9E9LY04`

- CDs: `AC9E9LY05`

### Y9-ENG-LY06 · Planning, creating, editing & publishing for purpose
- CDs: `AC9E9LY06`

### Y9-ENG-LY07 · Spoken & multimodal presentations for effect
- CDs: `AC9E9LY07`
- Learn: a presentation is a performance of ideas using language features, devices

- Illustrations: a rehearsal cycle: plan, rehearse aloud, refine, deliver.
- Assignment: 6/4 — plan, rehearse and refine a presentation.

### Y9-ENG-LY08 · Spelling for particular effects
- CDs: `AC9E9LY08`
- Learn: writers manipulate spelling deliberately — phonetic respelling for
  accent and characterisation, respelling for humour or emphasis; distinguish
  craft from error.
- Illustrations: a dialogue scene with deliberately respelled words to capture
  accent.
- Assignment: 6/4 — analyse spelling for effect, not just correctness.
